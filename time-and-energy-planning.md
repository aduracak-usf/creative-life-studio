# Time- and Energy-Aware Creative Planning

## Plan for the day the user has
Ask about time, energy, interest, skill level, and available materials only when those details would change the advice. Offer a useful starter idea when there is already enough information. A session is an invitation, not another obligation.

## Five minutes
Choose a palette, lay out a few supplies, sketch one shape, make a thread-and-fabric match, or write one next action. These are preparation or practice moments, not promises to finish a multi-step project.

## Fifteen minutes
Try a focused brush-mark exercise, make color swatches, practice a few straight stitches on scrap, arrange a small fabric palette, or prepare one project step. Leave time to tidy safely.

## Thirty minutes
Complete one bounded task such as sketching a composition, cutting prepared pattern pieces, sewing a short seam, or assembling a small paper craft. Stop at a natural checkpoint even if the whole project remains unfinished.

## Sixty minutes
Work on one substantial project segment, such as a first paint layer, assembling a simple sewn item, or finishing several related preparation tasks. Allow additional elapsed time for paint drying, ironing, or setup.

## Match activity to energy
- Low energy: choose colors, gather materials, look over a pattern, sketch, sort fabric, or do a small repetitive hand task.
- Medium energy: practice a new stitch, paint a simple subject, cut preplanned pieces, or complete one project stage.
- High energy: plan a more involved session if the available time, workspace, and experience also fit.

## Break down an unfinished project
1. Identify the last completed action.
2. Name the smallest safe next action.
3. Separate setup, making, drying, pressing, and cleanup time.
4. Estimate each step with a range and adjust after the first session.
5. End by leaving materials in a safe, easy-to-resume state.

## Be realistic and kind
Estimates vary by experience, workspace, tools, interruptions, and project complexity. Ask what amount of time feels comfortable, not what the user should accomplish. If a desired outcome will not fit, propose a smaller milestone rather than claiming the full project will fit.
