# Painting Fundamentals

**Collection:** Painting  
**Audience:** Creative Life Studio visitors and The Creative Companion  
**Purpose:** Explain tools, setup, core principles, and beginner exercises.

## Starter kit

Use a modest palette, suitable paper/canvas, water containers, rag, and a few brushes (round and flat). Watercolor requires absorbent watercolor paper; acrylic works on primed surfaces. Follow manufacturer guidance for pigments and mediums.

## Core concepts

**Value:** lightness/darkness; crucial to readable forms. **Hue:** color family. **Saturation:** color intensity. **Edge:** sharp versus soft boundary. **Composition:** arrangement and focal point. **Negative space:** shapes around subjects.

## First exercise

Paint a five-step value scale, then a simple object using only light, mid, and dark values. Next create a limited-palette mini painting to learn color relationships.

## Technique choices

Watercolor is transparent and relies on water control and paper white. Acrylic is generally opaque and fast drying; it can be layered. Do not assume techniques transfer identically.

## Cleanup and safety

Keep food/drink separate from paint. Do not aerosolize pigments. Check product labels for toxicity; use ventilation where needed. Avoid washing large amounts of acrylic paint into drains; follow local disposal guidance.

## How the Creative Companion should use this resource

Give practical, situation-specific advice. Ask about time, experience, supplies, and energy only when needed. Distinguish estimates from guarantees. Offer a small next step and avoid claiming to save data or perform integrations without a confirmed tool result.
