# Fabric Types and Properties

**Collection:** Sewing  
**Audience:** Creative Life Studio visitors and The Creative Companion  
**Purpose:** Help users select fabrics based on behavior, use, care, and equipment.

## Woven vs knit

**Woven** fabrics have intersecting warp/weft threads, usually limited stretch unless elastane is present. **Knit** fabrics have interlocking loops and typically stretch; they often require a ballpoint/stretch needle and stretch-capable seam.

## Common fabrics

**Quilting cotton:** stable, easy to cut and press; ideal for coasters, patchwork, bookmarks, and simple bags. **Canvas:** sturdy, good for totes, requires suitable needle. **Linen:** breathable, wrinkles and can fray; useful for napkins. **Denim:** durable, thick at intersections; use denim needle. **Jersey:** soft/stretchy; suitable for tees and scrunchies with appropriate technique. **Fleece:** warm and bulky, often low fray. **Silk/satin:** slippery, challenging for beginners. **Felt:** often does not fray, suitable for appliqué and crafts.

## Matching fabric to project

Coaster: stable cotton plus suitable batting. Tote: canvas or medium-weight cotton with interfacing if needed. Scrunchie: lightweight cotton or satin, depending on desired look. Pillow cover: woven cotton or linen blend. Avoid choosing stretchy fabric for a pattern drafted for stable woven fabric without adjustments.

## Care and prep

Check fiber labels and manufacturer instructions. Prewash when future laundering/shrinkage matters. Test colorfastness and iron temperature on scraps. For unknown vintage fabric, use cautious cleaning and test first.

## Shopping and substitutions

When exact fabric is unavailable, match stretch, weight, drape, thickness, and care needs—not just color. Tell the user when a substitute changes construction or finished appearance.

## How the Creative Companion should use this resource

Give practical, situation-specific advice. Ask about time, experience, supplies, and energy only when needed. Distinguish estimates from guarantees. Offer a small next step and avoid claiming to save data or perform integrations without a confirmed tool result.
