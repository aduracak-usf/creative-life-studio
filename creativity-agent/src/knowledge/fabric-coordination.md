# Fabric Coordination and Pattern Mixing

## Build a balanced combination
Choose one fabric to lead and let the others support it. A useful starting point is one larger-scale print, one smaller print or texture, and one solid. Repeat one or two colors across the fabrics so the group feels connected. Scale contrast helps patterns sit together without competing.

## Compare color and contrast
Place swatches side by side in the light where the finished item will be used. A shared color can tie two different prints together. A neutral solid can provide visual rest. For a calm result, keep contrast moderate; for a lively result, use stronger contrast in one smaller accent rather than everywhere.

## Visual coordination is not the same as material suitability
Color and print are aesthetic decisions. Fiber content, weave, stretch, weight, care requirements, and intended use determine whether fabrics behave well together. For a quilted item, choose fabrics and batting that can tolerate compatible washing and pressing. For a pouch, avoid pairing a very stretchy knit with a stable woven shell unless the pattern and construction account for the difference. Prewash washable fabrics if the project instructions recommend it.

## A quick swatch test
1. Select the lead print and identify two colors in it.
2. Add a smaller-scale print or texture that repeats at least one of those colors.
3. Place a solid next to the prints and check that it gives the eye a resting place.
4. Compare drape, thickness, stretch, fiber content, and care labels before cutting.
5. Take a quick photo in natural light and view the combination from a few steps away.

## Example: sage floral and cream cotton
A sage floral can pair with cream cotton if both fabrics have compatible weight and care requirements. Use the floral as the focal fabric, cream as a calm background or lining, and an optional solid sage accent. Check that the floral is not much lighter or stretchier than the cream cotton before sewing them into one seam.
