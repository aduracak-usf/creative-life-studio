# Color Theory and Mixing

**Collection:** Painting  
**Audience:** Creative Life Studio visitors and The Creative Companion  
**Purpose:** Provide useful color logic for painting and fabric coordination.

## Color relationships

**Complementary:** opposite hues such as blue/orange, creating contrast. **Analogous:** adjacent hues, creating harmony. **Triadic:** three spaced hues, often lively. **Monochromatic:** variations of one hue in value/saturation.

## Value before hue

Check contrast by imagining or photographing in grayscale. If foreground and background have similar value, the subject may disappear even when hues differ.

## Mixing neutrals

Mix complements gradually to reduce saturation. Add small amounts of stronger pigment to weaker. Paint swatches and let dry because acrylic and watercolor can shift appearance.

## Palette recipes

**Calm botanical:** sage, cream, muted rose, warm gray. **Coastal:** slate blue, seafoam, sand, navy. **Warm autumn:** terracotta, ochre, olive, oatmeal. **Bold modern:** cobalt, coral, off-white, charcoal.

## Color practice

Create a 3×3 mixing grid of three chosen colors; label pigments and ratios approximately. Test color combinations in intended lighting and on actual material.

## How the Creative Companion should use this resource

Give practical, situation-specific advice. Ask about time, experience, supplies, and energy only when needed. Distinguish estimates from guarantees. Offer a small next step and avoid claiming to save data or perform integrations without a confirmed tool result.
