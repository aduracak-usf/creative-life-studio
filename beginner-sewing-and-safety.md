# Beginner Sewing and Safety

## Useful beginner terms
- A seam allowance is the fabric between the raw edge and the stitching line. Check the pattern or project instructions rather than assuming one allowance fits every project.
- A straight stitch joins pieces; a backstitch at each end helps secure hand-sewn seams.
- Pressing means lifting and lowering an iron onto fabric. Sliding can stretch some fabrics.
- A running stitch passes a hand needle in and out at regular intervals.

## First machine session
Read the sewing machine manual for threading, needle insertion, bobbin winding, and presser-foot operation. Practice on two layers of scrap fabric before using the project pieces. Keep fingers away from the needle path, use the hand wheel only as directed, and switch the machine off before changing the needle or clearing a jam.

## First hand-sewing session
Use a blunt-ended embroidery needle for appropriate practice fabric when possible. Keep needles in a pin cushion or closed container, cut away from the body, and count pins before packing them away. Store scissors closed and out of reach of children.

## Beginner project: felt bookmark
Approximate active time: 30–45 minutes.

Materials: two felt pieces, needle, embroidery thread, scissors, ruler, and optional paper template.

1. Cut two matching bookmark rectangles, about 5 by 15 centimeters.
2. Stack the pieces and pin or clip away from the edge.
3. Thread the needle and secure the thread with a small knot.
4. Join the pieces with evenly spaced running stitches, keeping stitches a safe distance from the edge.
5. Secure the thread on the back and trim loose ends.

## Ironing and cutting safety
Use an iron on a stable, heat-safe surface and check the fabric care label before pressing. Rest the iron on its heel or designated stand. Unplug it after use and allow it to cool before storing. Use a rotary cutter only with its safety guard, a cutting mat, and the blade closed when not cutting.

## Fit and fabric cautions
Stretch knits, slippery satin, and very lightweight fabrics are harder to control than stable woven cotton. For a first project, choose a medium-weight woven cotton or felt and test stitches on scraps.
