# Painting Fundamentals and Color Theory

## Set up a small, manageable session
Choose a surface, two or three colors, a brush, water if the paint requires it, and a scrap for testing. Acrylic dries quickly and is generally used opaque; watercolor is transparent and needs water, absorbent paper, and room to dry. Follow the specific paint label, ventilate as directed, and keep art materials away from food.

## Simple color mixing
Start with a small amount of the lighter color and add a little of the darker color at a time. Make a test swatch before using a mix in the painting. A limited palette of one warm color, one cool color, and a neutral is easier to manage than many open paint pots. Adjacent colors on a color wheel often feel harmonious; complementary colors create stronger contrast and can look muddy if mixed together in equal amounts.

## Brush and mark practice
Use the side of a flat brush for broad marks and its edge for narrow lines. A round brush can make dots, lines, and small shapes. Practice pressure changes on scrap paper before starting. Rinse between colors and avoid leaving brushes resting in water.

## Beginner exercise: mini color study
Approximate active time: 15–30 minutes, plus drying time.

Materials: thick paper suitable for the paint, two or three paints or water-soluble colored pencils, a brush, water if needed, and a scrap for swatches.

1. Pick one simple subject such as a leaf, mug, or fruit.
2. Make three small palette swatches and adjust them before painting.
3. Sketch the largest shapes lightly or place them directly with a brush.
4. Add one mid-tone and one darker shadow shape.
5. Let the work dry as needed and note one color combination to try again.

## Realistic expectations
Drying time varies by paint, surface, humidity, and layer thickness. A short session can focus on planning, swatching, or one layer; it does not need to finish a painting.
